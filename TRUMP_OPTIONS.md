# Historical trump proposals

The current rule is a personal trump fixed by each participant's declared approach before drawing; see [RULES.md](RULES.md). The earlier shared-plurality rule and the alternatives below are superseded design notes.

All three keep fixed goals, simultaneous followers, mandatory follow-suit when possible, and one point per trick. They change how the played cards are compared. The comparison applies equally to players and the world.

| Option | Winner | Main benefit | Main cost |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **1. Highest value, any suit** | Highest effective value among all played cards | Simple comparison; no approach needs to dominate the scene | Zero-value statuses need a boost to beat ordinary world cards |
| **2. Changing trump** | Highest current trump; otherwise highest led-suit card | Preserves existing trump tactics while opportunities change | One changing suit to track; a suit can remain dominant for several tricks |
| **3. No trump, led suit only** | Highest card that follows the led suit | Few rules and strong control through leading | Being unable to follow guarantees a loss; Reposition cannot win off-suit |

## 1. Highest value, any suit — recommended first test

Follow suit if able. Once everyone has played, the highest **effective value** wins, regardless of suit. Apply Brace before comparing. A card with two suits through Revelation still has only one value.

For example, Strength 7 leads. A player with Strength must follow it. A player without Strength may play Intelligence 9 and beat the 7. Reposition lets someone choose that 9 even if they could follow Strength.

Suits still determine which cards you can play and which consolation a loss provides. Revelation lets a stronger card satisfy follow-suit through its borrowed suit. Your winning card advances your declared goal regardless of which suit you used.

This directly removes the need to declare one method strongest for the scene. Holding a bridge and translating an inscription can share a trick comparison without assigning either goal a trump suit.

The tradeoff is mechanical: every ordinary world card has a value above zero, so an unboosted status cannot win. Dazed still clears on a win, but usually needs a preceding Brace to get one. Reposition becomes a strong way to play a high card that following would otherwise block. High-value decks may dominate more often; follow-suit, the world hand, and the shared goal budget still need a playtest together.

This is not ordinary no-trump play: **off-suit cards can win**.

## 2. Changing trump — preserves the most existing interactions

Before dealing the world's N-card hand, reveal one extra world card as the first trump indicator. The indicator is not a hand card and awards no point. Follow suit as usual; highest trump wins, otherwise highest led-suit card wins. After each trick, the suit of the card the world just played becomes trump for the next trick.

The same current trump applies to every participant. No player has a personal trump and no goal is assigned a suit.

For example, the current trump is Weird. Strength 7 leads; Intelligence 9 played off-suit cannot win, but Weird 2 can. If the world played Strength this trick, Strength becomes trump for the next.

Fictionally, trump describes what the current opening favors. The party's goals stay fixed as the situation changes. A Strength opening can help someone support a collapsing roof while another character continues deciphering an inscription; the latter can keep working even if that moment favors someone else's contribution.

Reposition still enables off-suit trump plays. Revelation can supply trump, and an unboosted zero-value trump can beat non-trumps. The world card already exists on the table, so changing trump needs no new draw each trick.

The cost is that this still makes one suit stronger for a given moment. If even that conflicts with how you want to narrate simultaneous approaches, this does not solve the roleplaying objection. Leaders also influence the world's follow-suit reply, which influences the next trump; suit runs may persist rather than rotate evenly.

## 3. No trump, led suit only — simplest traditional comparison

Follow suit if able. Only cards matching the led suit can win; highest value among them wins. Revelation's borrowed suit can make a card eligible. Off-suit cards cannot win, regardless of value.

For example, Strength 7 leads. Strength 8 wins over it; an off-suit Intelligence 10 loses. A Strength 0 can win if it is the only eligible card.

No suit is privileged for the whole scene, and there is no trump marker. However, Reposition becomes a way to shed an unwanted card or conserve a led-suit card, not a way to win with an off-suit play. A player who cannot follow has no winning play unless Revelation supplies the led suit. Repeated leads in one suit can keep that player out of contention.

## What I would test

Test **option 1 against option 2** using the same goals, dealt hands, and ordered world hand. Reserve the same extra world card as option 2's opening indicator before either run. Let players make different choices as the rules demand.

Use a scene with different approaches: keep a gate open, decode an escape route, and calm the thing guarding it. Check whether card selection remains interesting, whether players can naturally narrate their own goals, and whether Dazed and the four consolations still earn their space.

No option has been playtested here. Option 1 is my recommendation for speed and approach freedom; option 2 is the stronger candidate if retaining trump interactions matters most.
