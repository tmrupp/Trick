# Joinery

An independent deck-building RPG prototype. A scene is a set of projects that players construct together. Cards are contributions to the fiction, and success comes from fitting them together precisely.

## Materials and character

Each player uses two standard decks. Aces are 1; ordinary cards run A–10. Jokers are unused. Spades mean force, Clubs finesse, Diamonds understanding, and Hearts connection or the uncanny. The GM needs no deck.

For a first test, use the 21-card starter: 3, 5, 7 of every suit; add A, 3, 5, 7, 9 of a primary suit and 2, 4, 6, 8 of a different secondary suit. Unused cards are your reserve. Face cards remain outside the deck as wound markers.

Your draw pile, hand, discard, and contributions to projects all belong to your character. Keep ownership clear when building a shared project. You can have at most the two physical copies of each ordinary card.

## Frame a scene

Name the goals before drawing. For each goal, write a project containing:

- **An exact total:** 10 for a small task, 15 for a substantial task, or 20 for a large task.
- **One or two required suits:** capabilities the result genuinely needs. A project may contain other suits, but must include each required suit to finish.
- **Success and failure:** what changes, who is exposed to harm, and whether failure causes one wound to each exposed character.

For example, rescuing someone from a locked, collapsing lift could require 15 and both Spades and Diamonds: support the weight and release the mechanism. The group can propose a different plan before the scene and negotiate a different project, such as Clubs and Hearts to reach and calm the passenger through a maintenance shaft.

Goals are not assigned to individual characters. Anyone can contribute to any project. Incompatible projects must have the same target total; establish their stalemate outcome in advance.

The scene lasts **four beats**, or three for urgent danger and five for extended work. Draw to five cards. Discuss the plan before drawing; during beats, choose independently and narrate the revealed results.

## Play a beat

Everyone secretly chooses one action and a target project, then reveals together:

- **Contribute:** play one ordinary card from hand to an unfinished project.
- **Withdraw:** identify one of your cards already in an unfinished project and return it to your discard. This makes room for a different contribution.
- **Retool:** discard up to two cards from hand.

Resolve all withdrawals first. Then add all contributions to each project simultaneously. There is no player order.

- **Below the target:** leave the new cards in the project.
- **Exactly on target:** if the required suits are present, complete the project. Otherwise it remains unfinished; someone will need to withdraw a card and replace it.
- **Over the target:** discard all cards newly contributed to that project this beat. Its earlier cards stay. The attempted work did not fit; narrate the clash without adding an extra penalty.

Refill each hand to five. When the draw pile empties, shuffle only your discard. Cards held in projects remain unavailable. If you cannot refill, continue with fewer cards; a player with no card can still Withdraw or Retool.

## Completion and opposition

A completed project takes effect immediately, and its cards stay committed until scene end. They cannot be withdrawn or recycled to solve another project. Describe how the contributions brought the result about.

The first completed project in an incompatible pair wins and closes the other. If both complete in the same beat, use the agreed stalemate and close both. Cards stay committed in either case. For these closed projects, apply only the failure consequences agreed for the losing or stalemated result.

Stop early when every project is resolved. Otherwise the final beat is the deadline: unfinished projects fail. Clean up by returning every player's hand and project cards to their own discard. Then apply the stated consequences.

## Wounds and death

A wound locks one ordinary card outside your deck: after cleanup, choose a card from your draw pile or discard and place it face-up under a face-card marker. It cannot be drawn, played, exchanged, or counted toward your twelve-card active minimum while wounded. Wounds may temporarily take you below that minimum.

**The third unhealed wound kills the character.** This is a visible three-card limit, not a discard scan or another check. A project can explicitly rescue or treat someone before a later failure would inflict the third wound. A completed treatment project heals one wound; return its ordinary card to discard and its marker to reserve. It cannot reverse death.

At a safe rest with care, heal all wounds. If a wound must be taken when no unlocked ordinary card remains, the character dies.

## Building the deck

After each consequential scene, each surviving player may make **one** change, win or lose:

- Add one ordinary reserve card to discard.
- Return one ordinary card from draw pile or discard to reserve.
- Exchange one ordinary card in those zones for any available ordinary reserve card, placing the replacement in discard.

Do not voluntarily reduce the unlocked ordinary deck below twelve cards. Wounded cards cannot be changed until healed. There are no experience points or suit-specific advancement rewards.

Deck-building is about useful combinations. Low cards finish nearly complete projects; high cards establish them quickly. Broad suits help satisfy different projects, while duplicate finishing values improve reliability. A larger deck retains more cards to draw while contributions are committed, but makes particular combinations less predictable.

## Worked beat

The lift project needs **exactly 15, with Spades and Diamonds**. It already contains 7 Spades and 3 Diamonds, totaling 10.

Two players reveal a 2 Clubs and a 3 Hearts. Together they supply the missing 5: the project completes, and those cards narrate finding a foothold and keeping the passenger calm.

If the reveals had instead been 2 Clubs and 5 Hearts, the new total would be 17. Both new cards go to their owners' discards; the original total remains 10. Another player could then supply the exact 5 on the following beat.

## What to test

Does hidden simultaneous contribution produce satisfying coordination or too many collisions? Are four beats enough for a mixed group of projects? Does committing cards make larger decks useful without encouraging everyone to add indiscriminately? This design accepts small sums and a visible tableau in exchange for removing trick comparison and an opposition deck.
