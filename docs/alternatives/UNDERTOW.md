# Undertow

An independent deck-building RPG prototype. You do not hold a hand of options. You enter a risky situation and reveal what your character can bring to it, deciding when to stop before their habits betray them.

## Materials and character

Each player uses two standard decks. Aces are 1; ordinary cards run A–10. Spades mean force, Clubs finesse, Diamonds understanding, and Hearts connection or the uncanny. Face cards are **Wounds**, regardless of suit; Jokers are unused. The GM needs no deck.

For a first test, use the 21-card starter: 3, 5, 7 of every suit; add A, 3, 5, 7, 9 of a primary suit and 2, 4, 6, 8 of a different secondary suit. Everything else begins in reserve. Start without Wounds.

You have a persistent draw pile and discard. Cards revealed during a run sit face-up in front of you until cleanup. They cannot be reshuffled during that run.

## Frame the stakes

Before revealing any cards, establish the available goals, their consequences, and their targets: **10 for a narrow result, 20 for a substantial result, 30 for a major result**. These are totals for a goal's whole group, not per-player requirements.

Each goal requires at least one card of a named suit among the contributions eventually banked for it. That suit describes a necessary part of the result, not a trump. Other suits contribute their full values. A rescue from crushing machinery might require Spades; recovering its hidden operating instructions might require Diamonds.

Each player chooses one goal for their run. Several players may choose the same goal. Set incompatible goals to the same target. State the outcome of a tied conflict in advance. Planning ends when the first cards are revealed.

## A run

Each active player simultaneously chooses **Push, Bank, or Abandon**. Announce choices together, then resolve them. On the first beat, everyone choosing to participate Pushes.

- **Push:** turn over the top card of your deck and add it to your run.
- **Bank:** commit the ordinary cards in your run to your chosen goal. Their values become secure progress. Your run ends.
- **Abandon:** discard your run at cleanup without contributing. Your run ends safely.

There is no discussion of choices during a run. Narrate what each reveal means: the 8 Spades is a forceful breakthrough, the 2 Clubs a small but useful adjustment.

An ordinary card whose **rank already appears among your ordinary run cards** causes a **bust**, even if its suit differs. Your run ends, none of its progress is banked, and you gain one Wound from reserve into discard. The repeated technique has overreached.

A Wound has no value and no suit for a goal. It does not cause a rank-repeat bust. However, **revealing a third Wound in the same run kills the character immediately**. One or two Wounds are warnings: you can still Bank or Abandon on the next beat. Neither action heals those Wounds.

After at most **six Pushes**, a surviving run that has not busted automatically Banks. A player who Banks, Abandons, busts, or dies takes no more actions this scene. They still narrate and watch their teammates decide whether to continue.

If your deck empties, shuffle your discard and continue. If both are empty, automatically Bank. Never shuffle cards already in the run or banked for a goal. Gaining a Wound does not itself reveal it or cause death; it becomes a danger when drawn in a later run.

## Resolve the scene

Once every run has ended, add the values banked for each goal. It succeeds if it meets its target and its required suit is present. Someone who banked no card of that suit can still support a teammate who did.

Among incompatible qualifying goals, the higher banked total wins; a tie gives the agreed stalemate. Compatible goals can all succeed. Apply the stated failure consequences; dangerous failure may add one Wound to each exposed survivor. Agree that cost before the run, rather than improvising damage afterward.

Return all revealed and banked cards to their owners' discards. Wounds remain in the deck. Cards belonging to a dead character do not contribute unless they had already been banked; a banked player has already ended their run and cannot die from later pushes that scene.

## Healing and deck-building

After each consequential scene, each surviving player may make **one** change, win or lose:

- Add an ordinary card from reserve to discard.
- Return any one card from draw pile or discard to reserve, including a Wound.
- Exchange an ordinary card in those zones for any available ordinary reserve card, placing the replacement in discard.

Keep at least twelve ordinary cards. At a safe rest with care, also return one Wound from anywhere in your deck to reserve. If the Wound reserve is empty, take no additional Wound; all available face cards are already circulating.

There is no universally strongest high-value deck. Loading up on 8s, 9s, and 10s raises progress per reveal but makes an early repeated rank more likely. Lower ranks buy diversity. Specializing in a suit helps satisfy relevant goals, but does not protect against repeated ranks. A larger deck can dilute Wounds; a smaller, carefully balanced deck can make its rank distribution more predictable.

## Worked run

Opening the floodgate requires **20 and at least one Spade**.

One player reveals 8 Spades, 3 Diamonds, and 5 Hearts: **16**, with three distinct ranks. They can bank useful progress or push toward completing the goal alone.

Their teammate has 2 Clubs and 4 Diamonds and Banks **6**. If the first player Banks too, the group reaches **22** with the required Spade and succeeds. If they independently choose to Push and reveal 8 Clubs, the repeated 8 busts: their 16 disappears, they gain a Wound, and the teammate's 6 is insufficient.

The group cannot negotiate this decision after revealing cards. Knowing when another character is likely to stop becomes part of working together.

## What to test

Does six pushes keep banked players involved without waiting too long? Do targets reward sharing risk rather than letting one specialist do everything? Are duplicate ranks frightening in a satisfying way? Is the third-Wound death threshold relevant often enough once a campaign has accumulated harm? This design trades direct card choice for fast decisions and suspense created by the player's own deck.
