Set up a check

  1. State your goal: what you're doing and how. The suit it leans on is your trump for the check.
  2. The GM sets its target from page 2 (you may suggest one), and the table agrees stakes.
  3. Size it:
    CheckTricksTargets
    Standard3 per player, +16–28, default 12
    Quick2 per player, +16–17, default 10
    Solo66–20, default 12
  4. Deal the world one card per trick from the GM's shuffled A–10 deck, face-up in a row. Everyone draws the same number.

Play a trick

  1. The world leads the next card in its row.
  2. Everyone chooses a card face-down, then all reveal together. No talk about plays. Hold a status in the world card's suit? Play it.
  3. Find the single highest card on the table, including the world card. Count any Brace.
  4. If that card is a player's, in the world card's suit or its owner's trump, it wins: its owner scores the world card's value.
  5. Tied players? World card's suit and own trump beats world card's suit only, which beats trump only.
  6. Otherwise the world takes the trick: the world card is highest, the highest card is off-suit, or the tie stays level.
  7. Everyone who didn't win takes their card's consolation, all at once.
Winning isn't optional. If your card is highest and on-suit, you take the world card, even if it busts you. To stay safe, play low, or let someone play over you.
High off-suit cards block. They can't win, but nobody beats them, so the world takes the trick. Use one to stop an ally busting.
Suits: Strength ♥ · Dexterity ♠ · Intelligence ♦ · Weird ♣. Ace = 1. J, Q and K are statuses, worth 0.

Consolations

From your losing card's printed suit, statuses included. They happen by themselves: the card you lose with is the choice. Brace, Shave and Mirror apply to your next play only: turn the losing card sideways as a reminder. No consolations after the final trick. The world has one too: when a player takes a world card, its suit changes the next world card. Brace counts the taken card's value higher, Shave scores 1 less, Intelligence Peeks (swap in the world deck's top card), Mirror counts 11 minus.

Example

Target 10. Ana (Strength trump) is on 2 and needs exactly 8. The world leads 6♦, and an 8♠ is next, but her best Heart is a 5. She plays 4♥, which loses, so she Braces for 4. Next trick she plays 5♥: it counts 5 + 4 = 9 for beating, takes the 8♠, and she scores the world card's 8: exactly 10. For the rest of the check she plays low.

Tally checks: statuses, special cases and targets

Statuses

Statuses are ordinary cards worth 0. They follow every rule, and their printed suit gives its consolation as usual. If the world card's suit matches a status in your hand, you must play it.

StatusWhen it losesIts own rule
InjuryBrace for 0, which does nothing.Two or more Injuries in your recent discard make survival a forced goal.
StumbleShave: your next win scores 1 less.At cleanup, a played Stumble goes on top of your deck, not to discard: you draw it first next check.
StressRecalculate: draw a card.Whenever you draw Stress, gain another Stress into discard.
CurseMirrors your next card.Breaking the compulsion gains one Stress per Curse outside your reserve.
  • A Braced status can win: a 0 Braced by 6 beats a world 5, and you score the 5.
  • Once you're on target, a status is a handy pass. Early on, it's a dead slot. Stumble costs a card every time it's played.
  • Critical and bust come from the fiction: what you were doing, and how. A bust's consequence may be a status; a Curse names its compulsion after what went wrong.
  • 4–6 short: the complication may be a status.

Special cases

  • Blessed (joker) always takes the trick and scores the world card. Two Blessed tie, so the world takes it. A played Blessed returns to reserve.
  • Survival is a forced goal with target 3 per Injury counted: 6 for two, 9 for three, 12 for four. A bust, or 7+ short, is death.
  • Incompatible goals: the better result wins; on the same step, the tally closer to its target. Equal results produce the agreed stalemate.
  • Empty hand: sit out the trick. Empty deck: shuffle your discard into a new one.

Playing well

  • Pick your prizes. Find world cards in the row that add up to your target.
  • Yield. If an ally needs this world card, play low. You still get a consolation.
  • Block. If this world card would bust an ally, play over them, even off-suit.
  • Set up. Brace to reach a big world card; Mirror to win with a low card, or lose safely with a high one; Shave to take a card one higher than you need.

Starter deck

Each player brings two standard decks. From one deck, take A, 2, 4, 5, 7, 8, 10 of your primary suit, A, 3, 6, 9, 10 of a different secondary suit, and 2, 3, 6, 9 and 4, 5, 7, 8 of the other two, your choice which: 20 cards, two of each value. Everything else, including jokers, is your reserve.
