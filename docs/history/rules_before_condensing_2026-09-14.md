# Trick — consolidated core rules

Current design reference · 14 September 2026

This is the single current reference for core play. It incorporates fixed goals, group checks, the agreed consolation actions, the original status effects, the new survival window, and evolution before reshuffle recovery. Earlier audit documents are historical proposals.

**Trump and the final card-comparison rule are still being workshopped.** Neither one shared trump for the whole check nor personal approach trumps is adopted here. Everything below describes the surrounding procedure; section 4 isolates that unresolved decision. Concrete defaults chosen to complete the draft are listed in section 12.

## 1. The character is the deck

Each player has a personal draw deck, hand, discard pile, and an area for cards played during the current check. Cards in hand, discard, and play remain part of that character's overall deck unless permanently removed.

| Suit | Approaches | Status |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Strength | Impose, endure, intimidate | Injury |
| Dexterity | Evade, deceive, improvise | Dazed |
| Intelligence | Analyze, persuade, plan | Stress |
| Weird | Intuit, empathize, invoke | Curse |

Suits describe approaches, not categories of permitted scenes. Any suit can be relevant to physical, social, investigative, or supernatural problems when the fiction supports it. Characters can pursue the same outcome through different approaches.

Build a starting deck of exactly **21 ordinary cards** using point buy. Values run from 1 to 10; each card costs its face value. Duplicate suit/value cards are allowed. There is no required suit balance. A budget of 105 points gives an average starting value of 5.

For this draft's maximum thirteen-card check, a character must retain at least **13 non-status cards**. No permanent removal may cross that floor. Status cards do not count toward it. This increases the original seven-card floor to support the larger hands; starting deck size and point-buy costs are unchanged.

The world's challenge deck contains the complete 1–10 set in each of the four suits: 40 cards. Shuffle it fresh for each check.

This core reference covers those four suits. Divine/Blessed and existing race and item extensions are not silently redefined by this consolidation; their interaction with the final group-resolution rule needs a separate compatibility pass.

## 2. Frame the check and commit to goals

A check covers a consequential situation. Use it when both success and failure would change what happens. Skip inconsequential actions. A hand can resolve an escape, negotiation, exploration obstacle, or confrontation; it need not represent one swing or one sentence.

Choose the check's size, **N**, before dealing:

| Size | Use |
| --- | --- |
| 7 tricks | A brief, focused situation |
| **11 tricks** | Default shared scene |
| 13 tricks | A broader situation with several moving parts |

N is the number of tricks played, not a difficulty rating. A larger budget permits more to be accomplished. Goal costs and consequences express difficulty and danger. N stays fixed for the check.

Before assigning goals, perform the **beginning-of-check survival scan** in section 8. Any rescue requirement is therefore known before anyone commits or draws.

Each player states **one goal** or commits to supporting another player's goal. Supporting means pursuing that same outcome through a described contribution. Record each goal, its participating characters, its trick cost, and its failure consequences.

**Goals, their costs, and participation are fixed for the whole check.** Players cannot change goals, move banked tricks, or redirect later wins to another goal. Completing a goal early does not allow its participants to switch to another. No rescue goals appear halfway through play.

Use these initial cost anchors; the GM sets the actual cost from the scope and opposition:

| Cost | Example scope |
| --- | --- |
| 1 trick | A narrow but consequential advantage |
| 2 tricks | A modest, bounded outcome |
| 3 tricks | A substantial outcome |
| 5 tricks | An ambitious outcome with several benefits |
| 6–N tricks | An exceptional undertaking |

Do not assign separate costs to every verb in a description. Identify the outcome the bid buys. The same goal's cost does not increase just because more players help.

The whole table shares at most N awarded tricks, including tricks taken by the world. If the combined desired costs exceed N, they cannot all be met. Make that tradeoff visible before commitments are finalized.

Failure consequences are declared before drawing. The ordinary consequence is an appropriate status for each exposed participant, together with the fictional setback; specify any different or additional consequence when framing the goal. A helper's exposure must also be clear. Losing a disagreement does not inherently mean taking physical Injury.

### Compatible and opposing goals

Several compatible goals may succeed in one check. Mutually exclusive goals contest the same outcome without requiring the characters to be enemies. For example, keeping a dangerous bell intact for study can oppose destroying it.

Identify mutually exclusive sets before dealing. Each bid still has its own minimum cost. At check end, ignore bids that failed to reach that cost. Among qualifying bids in a contested set, the highest total score wins. If the highest qualifying scores tie, apply the predeclared stalemate; neither contender secures its stated outcome. Compare total tricks, not points above the cost.

For this draft, frame each contested set as mutually exclusive. Rewrite tangled, partially overlapping outcomes into clear goals before play.

## 3. Deal and play together

After goals are fixed, every participant draws N cards from their personal deck. Draw in order, resolving initial-draw status effects as specified in section 6. If drawing requires a reshuffle, use section 7. No hand carries over from a previous check.

Deal N world cards face-up in a row. Their left-to-right order is fixed.

The group chooses the first player to lead before drawing, unless the fiction grants the world the first lead; announce that disadvantage during framing. Leading a trick controls the card lead, not a separate turn of fictional action.

For each of the N tricks:

1. The leader plays one card face-up, establishing the led suit.
2. The other players select legal cards face-down and reveal together. They must follow the led suit when able, subject to card effects.
3. The world plays its leftmost legal card. It follows suit if possible; otherwise it plays its leftmost remaining card. When it leads, it plays its leftmost card. Its response is deterministic and may be indicated as soon as the lead is known.
4. Compare the cards using the eventual comparison rule in section 4. If a player wins, add **one trick** to that player's fixed goal. If the world wins, no player goal gains a trick.
5. Resolve status destinations and any optional consolations. Players handle their own effects concurrently; finish them before the next lead.
6. Keep played cards in front of their owners in play order, unless an explicit effect sends a card elsewhere. The winner leads the next trick.

Only one point can be awarded from a trick. Several supporters beating the world do not produce several points. Use counters for goal scores so each player can retain their own cards.

Each player continues to play even after their goal reaches its cost. Further wins stay with that goal; they can matter to a contested score but do not purchase undeclared outcomes. Play all N tricks in this draft. There is no individual early exit.

Keep narration brief and responsive. A win shows progress toward the winning character's stated goal; a world win shows pressure or a setback. Characters can adapt their methods while pursuing the same fixed outcome. Settle the promised outcomes together at the end rather than giving fictional priority to whoever declared first.

### Ties and individual winners

Duplicate cards require a multiplayer tie rule. The concrete default is: if the leader is tied for the highest-ranked eligible card, the leader wins. Otherwise, if the highest-ranked cards tie, nobody wins, no point is awarded, and the current leader leads again.

In an unscored tie, each player has lost their individual trick for consolation and status purposes. This retains one identifiable winner when a point is awarded. The tie convention can be revisited alongside card comparison.

## 4. Trump and comparison — the remaining workshop

The original individual rules used a check-wide trump: highest trump won, otherwise highest led-suit card won; off-suit non-trumps could not win. That is the historical rule, **not a newly agreed group rule**.

The group procedure needs one comparison everyone can apply while characters pursue different routes. The user has rejected treating personal approach trumps as settled and does not find a single scene-wide trump satisfactory for roleplay. No approach suit is locked in by this reference.

The following constraints carry into the workshop:

- There is a shared trick budget and at most one awarded trick per round.
- Different goals and approaches coexist; one character's action does not define everybody else's fictional method.
- Brace changes value; Reposition waives follow-suit; Recalculate changes the hand; Revelation adds a second suit to the next play.
- Status cards retain their original effects and zero values. Their ability to win as zero-value trump is part of the old comparison and must be considered if trump is removed.
- No new comparison should require resolving each participant's bid as a separate private check.

Two useful next candidates are:

- **No trump:** keep follow-suit and let the highest led-suit card win. This removes the check's privileged approach, but Reposition can no longer win by allowing an off-suit trump, and zero-value statuses lose their trump role.
- **An opportunity that changes each trick:** use one public trump for the current beat, without assigning a suit to the whole scene or anyone's goal. One concrete mechanism to workshop is an extra world card revealed at setup to establish the first trump; afterward, the world's card from each completed trick sets trump for the next. A character keeps their goal, while changing conditions favor different resources. This retains one comparison per trick and needs no per-player trump ranking, but its fictional fit and feedback into world-card selection need testing.

Neither is adopted here. The opportunity mechanism uses an initial indicator card in addition to the world's N-card hand; its indicator is not played as a trick or awarded as a point. It is a candidate for discussion, not an additional core setup step.

This section is intentionally the only unresolved central procedure. The rest of the reference can be reviewed without pretending that a final group comparison has been selected.

## 5. Consolations

Consolations trigger on **individual trick loss**, not failure of a whole goal. The card's printed suit determines the effect, even if Revelation added another suit. All are optional. A player can lose a trick to an ally whose win still advances their shared goal; the original individual-loss trigger still applies.

### Strength — Brace

Stage the losing card outside your hand. Add its full value to your next played card, regardless of suit. Only one card may be staged; staging another discards the previous one. Discard the staged card after using the bonus, or at check end if unused. Do not draw a replacement for staging it.

### Dexterity — Reposition

On your next trick, you may ignore the requirement to follow suit. The normal comparison rule still determines whether that play can win. This permission expires after that play or at check end. It never changes another player's legal cards or the world's choice.

### Intelligence — Recalculate

Discard any one card from your hand, including a status, then draw one card from your deck. If there is no card left in hand to discard, you cannot use this effect. Use the normal reshuffle procedure if required. This mid-check draw does **not** duplicate Stress.

### Weird — Revelation

Choose one status card from your discard. Your next played card counts as that status's suit **in addition to its own**. Put the chosen status on top of your draw deck when you take the consolation; the borrowed suit remains available for your next play even if that top card moves again.

The extra suit can satisfy follow-suit and count as trump when the selected comparison rule includes trump. If the enhanced card leads, only its printed suit establishes the led suit. The borrowed suit expires after your next play or at check end. If your discard has no status to choose, you cannot take Revelation.

These actions affect the owner's cards and next play. They do not compete to control the world hand or revise an already resolved trick.

## 6. Status cards — original effects retained

Statuses enter through discard when gained. Each has value **0** and a fixed suit, regardless of what caused it. They legally follow suit. Under the original trump comparison, a zero-value trump beats every non-trump; that interaction remains an explicit consideration for section 4.

### Injury — Strength

Injury occupies a hand slot with a zero-value card. It has no on-draw or mandatory-play effect. Its survival rule is replaced by the top-N discard procedure in section 8. Injuries in hand, staged, or played in front do not count toward that window until they actually enter discard.

### Stress — Intelligence

Whenever a Stress card is drawn while forming the **initial check hand**, immediately place an identical copy in discard. The drawn card enters hand normally. Each initially drawn Stress produces its own copy; there is no once-per-hand cap.

Cards drawn later through Recalculate or another mid-check effect do not cause Stress to duplicate. An opening draw that continues after a reshuffle is still an initial draw. Survival checks have ordinary opening hands, so this rule applies to them too.

### Dazed — Dexterity

Dazed must be played whenever it is a legal play, including when you are free to play off-suit. It takes priority over all other cards. If several Dazed cards are legal, choose one.

If you win the trick with Dazed, permanently remove that card. If you lose, put it in discard. A teammate's win does not count as your Dazed winning. If you choose to use Reposition and that makes Dazed legal, its compulsory-play rule still applies.

### Curse — Weird

The GM proposes or co-authors a behavioral compulsion with the affected player. That player must approve it before the Curse enters their deck. It must have a clear, achievable fulfillment condition and agreed consequences for breaking it, usually another status.

A Curse cannot require unwanted player-versus-player behavior or cross an established table safety boundary. It has no duplication or other on-draw effect. Rest and reshuffle recovery cannot remove it. Only fulfilling its condition removes it.

No generic status replacement, Dread rename, separate curse subsystem, or pair-consuming recovery is introduced here.

## 7. Reshuffling, recovery, and evolution

### Reshuffle order

When a draw is required and your draw pile is empty:

1. **Check for and resolve available evolutions.**
2. **Remove one eligible status from discard**, if any. Curse is ineligible.
3. **Shuffle the remaining discard into a new draw pile**, then continue drawing.

Cards held in hand, played in front, or staged are not part of that discard shuffle. Do not combine an unexhausted draw pile with discard simply to prepare a larger hand.

Evolution is available during pre-check preparation. For a reshuffle required after trick play has begun, evolution is unavailable: proceed to the status-removal and shuffle steps. Do not bank a deferred evolution. This concrete timing default keeps advancement before a check while preserving Recalculate's draw effect.

### Rest

At a GM-granted rest scene, remove any number of non-Curse statuses from your discard. Rest does not remove statuses elsewhere in the deck or fulfill Curse conditions. A triggered survival check happens immediately; do not insert a rest before resolving it.

### Evolution qualification

Before reshuffle recovery, inspect **the discard about to be shuffled**. Each suit with two or more matching statuses qualifies, unless that suit is latched. Several suits can qualify independently. Each evolution is optional.

Evolution does **not** consume the qualifying statuses. Retain the original latch rule: after resolving an evolution, mark that suit as latched. It cannot evolve again while the character still owns at least two statuses of that suit. Clear the latch as soon as the total across the personal deck falls below two. Moving a status between hand, play, draw pile, and discard does not clear it. Removing a status can.

Declining evolution does not latch the suit. Recovery happens after qualification and resolution, so it cannot remove a qualifying card before the evolution opportunity is checked. It can clear a latch afterward if ownership falls below two.

Keep ordinary-card removals above the thirteen-card floor. If an evolution removes an ordinary card from a partially dealt opening hand, finish drawing back to N afterward.

### Evolution rewards

All suits have **Add**: draw three cards from that suit's acquisition pile, keep one, and return the other two. The kept card joins discard and will enter the new shuffled deck. Each acquisition uses a complete 1–10 set; keeping a card does not deplete future opportunities.

| Suit | Original alternative retained |
| --- | --- |
| Strength — Purge the Weak | Permanently remove the lowest-value ordinary card in your personal deck. Choose among ties. Choose this or Add. |
| Intelligence — Triage | Permanently remove any one ordinary card from your personal deck. Choose this or Add. |
| Dexterity — Redirected | Add and removal of any one ordinary card are independently optional: choose either or both. Choosing neither does not resolve an evolution. |
| Weird — The Bargain | Remove one ordinary card and convert it into a Curse in your discard. The GM and affected players agree on a group compulsion; put an identical Curse into every other player's discard. One agreed condition lifts that compulsion for everyone. Choose this or Add. |

Status effects and Curse eligibility are unchanged. In particular, there is no option to consume two Curses to remove their obligations.

## 8. Survival and rescue — beginning and end only

### The window and difficulty

Inspect the top **N** cards of the affected player's discard, where N is the current or just-completed check's size. If discard contains fewer than N cards, inspect all of it. Do not draw or shuffle to fill the window.

Count **M Injuries** in that window:

- **M < 2:** no survival requirement.
- **M ≥ 2:** rescue requires **M tricks**.

Only Injuries actually in that discard window count. Injuries in hand or played in front do not. Other statuses do not add difficulty. There is no base cost, three-card reveal scan, temporary survival deck, or loaned eighth card.

### Beginning of a check

Choose N, then inspect discard **before reshuffling, removing statuses, or drawing**. Any required rescue is known while goals are being set. A character's rescue is a goal with that character as its beneficiary and a cost of M.

Players may commit to the rescue or to another goal. The endangered player can contribute to their own rescue. Helpers need no separate check or card loan. All participants commit before the hand begins and remain with their chosen goal.

Record M once. Subsequent card movement, evolution, or recovery does not cancel or reprice that requirement. If the check was already triggered by the preceding end scan, use that recorded rescue requirement rather than creating a duplicate or recounting it.

### During a check

**Do not scan for survival or introduce rescue goals during trick play.** Recalculate, Revelation, Stress copies, status removal, and other discard changes affect the pile, but their survival consequences are assessed at the end.

This makes every goal and rescue requirement fixed for the whole hand. No player has to abandon a declared goal because of a newly introduced mid-check rescue.

### End of a check

1. Resolve all goal scores. A character whose required rescue did not reach its recorded M **dies**. Ordinary failure costs cannot replace that death. A completed rescue lets its beneficiary survive this check.
2. For surviving characters, clean up cards in the fixed order in section 9 and apply the check's consequences, including newly gained Injuries.
3. Inspect the top N discard cards for each surviving character. If any window contains two or more Injuries, record its M and **immediately begin another shared check** with those rescue requirements.

The immediate survival check uses the same N as the check that triggered it. Each required rescue is a separate goal. Available allies can join a rescue or pursue other current goals, declaring their commitments before drawing. Previously settled outcomes are not reopened.

Failure to meet a rescue cost at the end of its hand means death. No special suit or individual turn is imposed by survival; it uses the eventual shared comparison rule like any other check.

### Repeat checks

The end scan also applies after a successful survival check. Success does not remove Injuries or grant immunity. If the **new post-cleanup window** still contains two or more Injuries, another immediate survival check follows. This preserves the literal beginning/end trigger and the original possibility of repeated survival, with the new simple count replacing the old threshold scan.

Do not repeatedly re-scan an unchanged pile while setting up a single check. One recorded requirement per endangered character belongs to that check. The next end scan occurs only after its completed hand, cleanup, and consequences.

Since M counts Injuries within N cards, each individual rescue cost fits within a full N-trick hand. Several rescue goals and other goals may together demand more than N tricks; the group must decide its priorities before dealing.

## 9. Card destinations and cleanup

Each player retains their ordinary played cards in a visible left-to-right row, earliest play first. Explicit destinations happen when their effects say so: Recalculate discards immediately, losing Dazed goes to discard, winning Dazed is removed, Brace is staged then discarded, and Revelation moves its selected status onto the draw pile.

At check end, use this order:

1. Put any unplayed hand cards onto discard. In the core full-hand procedure there are normally none; for extra-card effects, use fixed suit order Strength, Dexterity, Intelligence, Weird, with ascending value within each suit.
2. Discard any unused staged Brace card, then the played row from earliest to latest play. Cards already discarded or removed are not moved again.
3. Add newly gained consequence statuses on top. If several types are gained together, place non-Injuries first and Injuries last. Among non-Injuries use Dexterity, Intelligence, Weird order.
4. Perform the end-of-check survival scan.

Do not freely arrange the discard pile. The top-N survival window depends on order. Newly gained Injuries are on top so the result of the check participates in that window.

Status copies or cards already discarded during play remain where those earlier events placed them. Recalculate and Revelation can affect the eventual window through their printed effects; cleanup does not undo or freely reorder those effects.

## 10. Worked group check

An eleven-trick scene has three players. Before goals are declared, Mara's top eleven discard cards contain two Injuries, so her rescue costs two tricks.

Mara commits to surviving. Ivo commits to supporting her rescue. Sen commits to getting the expedition's records out, at a cost of three. These commitments are fixed before anyone draws.

Mara wins one trick, Ivo wins two, Sen wins three, and the world wins five. The totals use all eleven tricks. Mara's rescue scores three and succeeds; Sen's records goal scores three and succeeds. Ivo's extra rescue point could not be redirected to another goal.

After cleanup and consequence cards, inspect the new top-eleven windows. If Mara's window now contains fewer than two Injuries, no further survival is triggered. If it contains two, she faces an immediate new survival check costing two; if it contains three, the cost is three.

If Recalculate had moved an Injury into a qualifying discard window halfway through the scene, it would not have introduced rescue then. That changed pile would only matter at the end scan.

For a nonviolent contest, one group might bid to preserve the bell and another to destroy it. Both goals can reach their individual costs, but only the qualifying bid with the higher total succeeds. A tie produces the stalemate agreed before drawing.

## 11. Table procedure at a glance

**Choose N → scan discard windows → declare fixed goals and support → deal, evolving before recovery when an eligible reshuffle is needed → play N shared tricks → resolve goals and deaths → fixed cleanup and consequences → scan again.**

The world hand can be operated by any player or the GM. Its plays are deterministic. There is no rotation of separate character checks; everyone in the scene participates in the same hand.

## 12. Concrete drafting defaults and the workshop boundary

The following operational choices make this reference specific where the conversation had not selected a final value or edge rule. They are defaults for review, not claims that they were separately approved:

- Eleven tricks by default, with seven/thirteen presets; thirteen ordinary cards as the minimum deck floor.
- Initial goal-cost anchors of one, two, three, and five tricks.
- Original individual-loss consolation eligibility and original evolution rewards/latches; discard-only evolution qualification.
- Evolution only during pre-check preparation; mid-play reshuffles do not defer advancement.
- Removal of one eligible status after evolution when reshuffling; Curse remains ineligible.
- One winner per trick, with leader-priority ties and otherwise unscored ties; contested goals compare qualifying total scores.
- Fixed cleanup order, fixed rescue costs, and same-N immediate survival hands.
- Repeated end-of-check survival scans, including after successful survival, with no added immunity rule.

Trump/card comparison is explicitly unresolved in section 4. This document is a consolidated design reference, not a claim of a fully tested game or a finalized comparison engine.
