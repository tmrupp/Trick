TRICK-TAKING RPG — CORE RULES REFERENCE
========================================


1. CORE CONCEPT
---------------
Challenges are resolved through trick-taking. Each player holds a personal deck
played against an open hand (the world's opposition). The player draws 7 cards
when starting a challenge. Trump suit is set by the challenge type. Taking all
7 tricks is severe difficulty; taking all 7 while the open hand leads is
legendary. Losing challenges adds status cards to your deck. The deck is the
character.


2. THE FOUR SUITS
-----------------
  Suit          Approach                         Status Card   Trump When
  Strength      Impose, endure, intimidate        Injury        Strength challenge
  Dexterity     Evade, deceive, improvise         Dazed         Dexterity challenge
  Intelligence  Analyze, persuade, plan           Stress        Intelligence challenge
  Weird         Intuit, empathize, invoke         Curse         Weird challenge

Suits describe how a character approaches a problem, not what kind of scene is
allowed. A social, physical, investigative, or supernatural action can use any
suit when the described approach supports it.

The player leads the first trick unless the narrative grants disadvantage.
The open hand plays the leftmost valid card and must follow the led suit.
Highest trump wins. If no trump is played, the highest card of the led suit
wins. Off-suit non-trump cards cannot win.


3. STATUS CARDS
---------------
Status cards enter via the discard pile. All have value 0. They can follow suit
and can win a trick as trump (value 0 beats all non-trump cards). Each suit's
status type is fixed regardless of source.
At reshuffle, the player may remove up to one status card (not Curse) before
shuffling.

INJURY (Strength)
  - Clogs the hand. No additional mechanical effect.
  - Outside of a challenge, if 2+ Injury cards appear in the top 7 cards of
    the player's discard, a survival event fires immediately (see §8).

STRESS (Intelligence)
  - When drawn on the initial challenge hand draw, immediately places an identical copy
    in the discard. Growth is exponential.
  - Mid-challenge draws (e.g. via Recalculate) do not trigger this.

DAZED (Dexterity)
  - Must be played whenever it is a legal play — including when playing
    off-suit freely. Takes priority over all other cards in hand.
  - On trick win: permanently removed from the game.
  - On trick loss: goes to the player's discard.

CURSE (Weird)
  - The GM proposes or co-authors a behavioral compulsion with the affected
    player. The player must approve it before the Curse enters their deck.
  - A compulsion cannot require unwanted player-versus-player behavior or cross
    an established table safety boundary.
  - Each Curse has a clear, achievable fulfillment condition. Breaking the
    compulsion has an agreed narrative consequence, usually adding a status.
  - Cannot be removed by rest or reshuffle. Only by fulfilling the condition.


4. TRICK LOSS CONSOLATION ACTIONS
----------------------------------
Triggered on trick loss only (not challenge loss). The consolation is determined
by the suit of the card the player played, not the suit that won. All optional.

BRACE (Strength)
  Stage the lost card outside hand. Its value adds to your next played card
  regardless of suit. One staged card at a time. Staging a new card discards
  the previous. Discarded after use or at end of challenge if unused.

REPOSITION (Dexterity)
  Choose which valid card the open hand plays next trick instead of the
  leftmost-valid default. No effect if only one valid play exists.

RECALCULATE (Intelligence)
  Discard any one card from hand (including status cards) and draw one from
  the top of your deck.

REVELATION (Weird)
  Choose 1 status card from your discard. Your next played card counts as
  that status card's suit in addition to its own. Then place that status
  card on top of your deck. The additional suit can satisfy follow-suit and
  counts as trump when applicable. If the enhanced card leads, only its printed
  suit establishes the led suit.


5. RECOVERY
-----------
REST (GM-granted narrative scene)
  Remove any number of status cards from your discard, player's choice.
  Curse cards cannot be removed by rest.

RESHUFFLE (when deck empties)
  The player may remove up to one status card from the discard before
  shuffling (Curse ineligible). Check for optional deck evolution triggers.
  Then shuffle.

DAZED (on play): permanently removed if the trick is won; goes to discard if lost.
CURSE: only removed by fulfilling its agreed condition.


6. DECK CONSTRUCTION
--------------------
Built at campaign start via point buy. Finished size: exactly 21 cards.
Card values run 1–10 per suit. Each card costs points equal to its face value.
No suit balance requirements. Budget is GM-determined.
Budget guidance: 105 points yields an average card value of 5 across 21 cards.
Duplicate copies of the same suit+value card are permitted.
A personal deck must always contain at least 7 non-status cards. No effect may
remove a non-status card if doing so would leave fewer than 7. Status cards do
not count toward this floor.

7. DECK EVOLUTION
-----------------
After the reshuffle removal, check the player's entire personal deck. Each suit
with 2+ status cards of that suit qualifies for evolution independently unless
that suit is latched. Multiple suits can qualify during one reshuffle. Each
qualifying evolution is optional: the player may resolve it or decline it. If
the player resolves an evolution, choose one currently available option for
that suit unless the option explicitly allows multiple effects. An optional
evolution may still have only one available choice.

After resolving an evolution, mark that suit as latched. It cannot evolve again
while 2+ status cards of that suit remain. Clear the latch as soon as the count
falls below 2; the suit can qualify again if its count later returns to 2+.
Declining an evolution does not latch it, so the opportunity remains available
at later reshuffles while the suit still qualifies.

All suits share the ADD option:
  Draw 3 cards from that suit's acquisition pile; keep 1, return 2.
  The kept card goes to the discard pile and is shuffled into the new deck.
  The acquisition pile is a complete 1–10 set each time; keeping a card does
  not deplete it for future evolutions.

STRENGTH — Purge the Weak
  Remove the lowest-value non-status card in the deck permanently.
  Ties: player chooses.

INTELLIGENCE — Triage
  Remove any one non-status card freely (any suit, any value).

DEXTERITY — Redirected
  Add and Remove are independently optional. The player may use either, both,
  or neither.
  Remove: any one non-status card freely.

WEIRD — The Bargain
  Remove one non-status card from the deck. The GM and affected players agree
  on a group compulsion for the removed card, converting it into a Curse. Place
  an identical Curse copy into every other player's discard simultaneously.
  One clear, achievable condition agreed by the group lifts the compulsion for
  all players at once.


8. THE SURVIVAL EVENT
---------------------
TRIGGER
  Outside of any challenge: 2+ Injury cards are found in the top 7 cards of
  the player's discard. The survival event fires immediately. The top 7 scan
  is the trigger condition only.

THRESHOLD SCAN
  1. Combine the injured player's draw pile and discard, then shuffle them into
     a temporary survival deck. This setup is not a reshuffle: do not remove a
     status card or check deck evolution.
  2. The survival threshold begins at 2 tricks.
  3. Draw and reveal up to 3 cards from the survival deck and set them aside.
     Revealed status cards do not trigger their on-draw effects.
  4. If the revealed group contains 1+ status cards, increase the threshold by
     1 and reveal another group. If the group contains no status cards, stop.
  5. Stop when the threshold reaches 7 or no cards remain to reveal.
  6. Recombine and shuffle the revealed and unrevealed cards. This becomes the
     player's draw deck for the survival challenge.

SEQUENCE
  1. Resolve the threshold scan and announce the resulting threshold.
  2. The injured player draws a new 7-card hand.
  3. Up to one other player may lend one card from their discard. The lent card
     joins the injured player's hand, creating an 8-card hand for this challenge.
  4. Run a Strength challenge at the announced threshold. Normal trick loss
     consolation actions apply.
  5. SUCCESS: The injured player's played and remaining hand cards go to their
     discard as normal. The lent card is returned to its owner.
  6. FAILURE: The lent card is returned to its owner, then the character dies.

LENDING A HAND
  Up to one player may lend per event, after the threshold is announced. The
  lent card comes from the helper's discard and joins the injured player's
  starting hand after they draw 7 cards. The loan rule overrides the card's
  normal destination: it returns to the helper's discard after the check,
  whether the injured player succeeds or fails.

RETRIGGER
  After a successful survival challenge, the injured player's played and
  remaining cards are discarded, resetting the top 7. Re-evaluate the trigger
  on this new top 7. If 2+ Injuries remain, another survival event fires
  immediately.


9. CHALLENGE STRUCTURE
----------------------
FRAMING
  Challenge type: Player suggested, GM declared.
  Trump suit: Determined by the declared challenge type.
  Threshold: Declared publicly before play begins. Outcome is binary.
  The player draws a new 7-card hand when the challenge starts. Survival events
  are challenges and follow this rule. No hand is kept between challenges.
  Individual card effects such as Recalculate can still instruct a player to
  draw during a challenge.
  At the end of a challenge, the player places all of their played cards and
  all cards remaining in hand into their discard in any order they choose,
  unless a rule gives a card a different destination. On failure, add the
  appropriate status card on top after the player finishes ordering the other
  cards.

MID-CHALLENGE RESHUFFLE
  If the player's deck empties during a challenge, reshuffle immediately:
  remove up to one status card (Curse ineligible), check optional evolution,
  shuffle, then continue the challenge with the refreshed deck.

TRICK RULES
  The winner of the previous trick leads the next.
  Ties are won by the leader of that trick.
  If the player has no cards of the led suit, they may play any card freely.

ENDING A DECIDED CHALLENGE
  Once the player has taken the threshold number of tricks, success is
  guaranteed. Once their wins plus the remaining tricks are fewer than the
  threshold, failure is unavoidable. In either case, the player may end the
  challenge immediately or finish playing if an unresolved card effect matters.
  If the challenge ends immediately, unplayed cards trigger no on-play effects
  and all cards are discarded using the normal end-of-challenge procedure.

THE OPEN HAND
  7 cards from a 40-card challenge deck (values 1–10 in all four suits).
  Reshuffled fresh at the start of each challenge. Face-up at all times.
  Always plays the leftmost valid card and must follow the led suit. If the
  open hand has no card of the led suit, it plays the leftmost card freely.
  The player leads the first trick unless the narrative grants disadvantage.

DIFFICULTY GUIDE
  1 trick   Trivial    (skip if failure is uninteresting)
  2 tricks  Trivial
  3 tricks  Easy
  4 tricks  Routine
  5 tricks  Moderate
  6 tricks  Hard
  7 tricks  Severe

LEGENDARY CHALLENGE
  Require all 7 tricks and have the open hand lead the first trick. Use
  sparingly and make both the danger and the reason for disadvantage clear
  before play begins.


10. PLAY AT THE TABLE
---------------------
WHEN TO CALL A CHALLENGE
  Use a challenge for a consequential turning point where both success and
  failure would change the scene. Do not call a full challenge for every swing,
  lockpick, or individual sentence in a conversation.

FICTIONAL BEATS
  1. LEAD: The active player describes their approach.
  2. TRICK WIN: The active player describes progress or improved position.
  3. TRICK LOSS: The GM or open-hand operator introduces a complication.
  4. CONSOLATION: The active player describes how they adapt to the setback.
  5. CHALLENGE RESULT: The GM resolves the consequence for the whole scene.
  Keep each trick's narration brief so the fiction supports rather than pauses
  the card play.

OPEN-HAND OPERATOR
  At a multiplayer table, the player to the active player's left operates the
  open hand and announces its deterministic plays. They make no choices unless
  a rule such as Reposition instructs them to do so. They may narrate trick-loss
  complications with the GM's guidance. The GM operates the hand when no other
  player is available.

PLAYTEST TRACKING (optional)
  During development play, record the number and real-time length of challenges,
  status counts at reshuffle, deliberate status retention for evolution, and
  whether survival events feel dangerous. Also note when inactive players lose
  engagement. Review these observations before changing individual card effects.


GLOSSARY
--------
Acquisition Pile
  A complete set of values 1–10 for one suit, recreated for each Add evolution.
  Keeping a card does not deplete future acquisition piles.

Brace
  The Strength consolation. Stage the lost card to add its value to your next
  played card regardless of suit.

Challenge
  The core resolution unit. A new hand of 7 cards is played as tricks against
  the open hand. A survival loan can temporarily add an eighth card. The player
  may end early once success is guaranteed or failure is unavoidable.

Consolation Action
  An optional benefit taken when losing a trick, determined by the suit of the
  card the losing player played. See §4.

Curse
  The Weird-suited status card. Carries a behavioral compulsion approved by the
  affected player and a clear, achievable fulfillment condition. It cannot
  require unwanted player-versus-player behavior or cross a table safety
  boundary. Cannot be removed by rest or reshuffle; only by fulfilling its
  agreed condition.

Dazed
  The Dexterity-suited status card. Must be played whenever legal, including
  when playing freely off-suit. On trick win: permanently removed from the
  game. On trick loss: goes to discard.

Deck
  A player's personal card pool, representing their character. Persists between
  sessions and evolves through play. It must always contain at least 7
  non-status cards.

Deck Evolution
  An optional process by which a player's deck changes at reshuffle when 2+
  status cards of the same suit remain after reshuffle recovery. Each suit
  qualifies independently and offers one or more options. A resolved suit
  latches until its status count falls below 2. See §7.

Discard
  The face-up pile of cards a player has played or discarded during play.
  At challenge end, the player orders their played and unplayed cards in it as
  they choose; a failure status is then placed on top. Shuffled back into a new
  deck at reshuffle.

Follow Suit
  Playing a card of the same suit as the led card. Required when possible.
  The open hand must always follow suit if able.

Hand
  The cards a player holds during a challenge. A new 7-card hand is drawn at
  the start of each challenge; no hand persists between challenges. A survival
  loan can temporarily create an 8-card hand.

Injury
  The Strength-suited status card. Clogs the hand with no other effect. If 2+
  appear in the top 7 of the discard outside a challenge, a survival event
  fires.

Lead
  The act of playing the first card of a trick, which sets the suit that other
  players must follow. The player leads unless the narrative grants disadvantage.

Lending a Hand
  After a survival threshold is announced, up to one other player may lend a
  card from their discard. It joins the injured player's 7-card hand and returns
  to the helper's discard after the check, regardless of the outcome.

Open Hand
  7 cards drawn from the 40-card challenge deck, placed face-up. Represents
  the world's opposition. Always plays the leftmost valid card and must follow
  suit. At a multiplayer table, it is normally operated by the player to the
  active player's left.

Recalculate
  The Intelligence consolation. Discard any one card from hand and draw one
  from the top of your deck.

Reposition
  The Dexterity consolation. Choose which valid card the open hand plays next
  trick instead of the leftmost default.

Reshuffle
  When the player's deck empties, the discard becomes the new deck. Before
  shuffling: remove up to one status card (not Curse), then check optional
  evolution triggers.

Rest
  A GM-granted narrative scene. Remove any number of status cards from the
  player's discard. Curse cards cannot be removed. Primary recovery mechanism.

Revelation
  The Weird consolation. Choose a status card from your discard; your next
  played card also counts as that status card's suit, then put it on top of
  your deck. The added suit can satisfy follow-suit and count as trump, but the
  card's printed suit alone establishes the led suit.

Status Card
  Any of the four negative cards (Injury, Stress, Dazed, Curse) that enter via
  the discard pile. Value 0. Can follow suit; can win as trump (beats all
  non-trump despite value 0).

Stress
  The Intelligence-suited status card. When drawn on the initial hand draw,
  immediately places an identical copy in the discard. Mid-challenge draws
  (e.g. via Recalculate) do not trigger this.

Survival Event
  An event that fires outside any challenge when 2+ Injury cards appear in the
  top 7 of the player's discard. Its threshold starts at 2 and rises by 1 for
  each consecutively revealed 3-card group containing a status. The player then
  draws a new 7-card hand and runs a Strength challenge. Failure means death.
  See §8.

Threshold
  The number of tricks required to succeed at a challenge. Declared publicly
  by the GM before normal play begins; survival thresholds are generated by the
  survival scan.

Trick
  A single round of card play: one card from each side. The higher trump wins;
  if no trump, the highest card of the led suit wins. Off-suit non-trumps cannot
  win. Ties go to the leader.
  The winner of each trick leads the next.

Trump
  The suit that beats all other suits in a trick. Set by the declared challenge
  type and fixed for the duration of that challenge.
