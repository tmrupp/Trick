# Item drafts

Nine new items, drafted 2026-09-23 to 2026-09-25 and not yet in [item_catalog.js](item_catalog.js). Card wording follows [README.md](README.md); design guidance lives in [workshop.md](workshop.md). When one is approved, move its lines into the catalog and delete it here.

They balance the suits. Before them the set held four Strength, four Dexterity, three Intelligence, and three Weird trinkets, and three Strength relics against two of each other suit. After them, in Strength / Dexterity / Intelligence / Weird order: trinkets 5 each, relics 3 each.

Each item does two jobs. **In the fiction** it does one concrete, meaningful thing a character would seek it out for, printed on the card as the first sentence of its Effect. **At the table** it pulls a lever no current item touches:

| Lever | Core rule it bends | Item |
|---|---|---|
| Incompatible goals | The greatest margin wins; a tie gives the agreed stalemate | Tally Stick |
| Other players' hidden hands | Hands are private; no signals once drawing begins | Hag Stone |
| A card carried between checks | Every hand is discarded at cleanup | Bindle |
| The world's place at the table | The world plays every trick | Hand of Glory |
| Taking a play back | A card cannot be taken back once played | Backward Watch |
| Pooling wins | Wins are never pooled or transferred | Handfasting Cord |
| The world's hand size | The world is dealt N cards | Bone Whistle |
| Goal cost | Costs are set before drawing and stay fixed | Letter of Introduction |
| Failure consequences | Each exposed participant gains a fitting status | Madstone |

Tabletop sources, re-rooted in hollow folk magic: the Bag of Holding and Robe of Useful Items (the bindle), the Gem of Seeing (the hag stone, itself real folklore), the historical burglar's Hand of Glory, and time-turning devices from many RPGs (the watch). The tally stick and handfasting cord come straight from folk practice.

## Tally Stick — Intelligence trinket

> *A notched stick split lengthwise; whoever holds the other half keeps to what is notched.*
> Wake: Cut it from the fence post of a farm that changed hands in a quarrel, and carve your name on both halves.
> When: Your approach is Intelligence.
> Effect: Notch your terms and put the other half in someone's hand: they keep to those terms as if they had agreed. Before drawing, name a player whose goal is incompatible with yours and notch a settlement, approved by the GM, that lets both goals partly succeed; if both meet their costs, it happens instead of either goal.
> Cost: Gain 1 Stress. Whatever is notched binds you as well.
> *People bring you their quarrels unasked, and some cross the road to avoid you.*

**In the fiction.** Tallies were how the hollow kept debts and bargains honest before anyone could read. This one does not wait for agreement: carve the terms, press the other half into a hand, and the creditor forgives the debt, the neighbour gives up the field, the feud is over whether they liked it or not. It is the quarrel-ender people fear as much as they need.

**At the table.** Incompatible goals are the table's only real conflict, and today one side must lose. You write the compromise and the GM approves it before drawing, as with any goal, so nothing is invented at resolution and no other player has to agree: if both costs are met, the notched settlement is what happens. If only one is met, goals resolve normally.

**Watch.** Forcing terms on anyone is a lot for a trinket, so the limiters are physical and social: the other half must be put into their hand, the terms bind you too, and the GM approves any settlement at the table. It only matters at the table when two goals clash.

Second pass: the first version had the GM invent a compromise at resolution; the second had both players negotiate it. Now the holder imposes it, which is simpler to run and is the stick's whole job.

## Letter of Introduction — Intelligence trinket

> *A letter in a respected hand; whoever reads it hears you out and meets you halfway.*
> Wake: Do a service for someone the town respects, and ask them to write it for you.
> When: Your approach is Intelligence.
> Effect: Show it and you are heard: a clerk finds your file, a banker extends credit, a stranger lets you in. Before drawing, your goal costs 1 less, minimum 1. Survival costs never change.
> Cost: Gain 1 Stress. The signer hears what you did in their name, and holds you to it.
> *People expect you to speak for your patron.*

**In the fiction.** A town that runs on pacts and reputation runs on who will vouch for you. The letter gets you a hearing with the mine office, credit at the store, a seat at a Varn's table, a room when the boarding house is full. It carries a real person's name, so it is only as good as their standing and your conduct.

**At the table.** The only item that touches goal cost. One trick less is a large gift: a modest goal becomes narrow, a substantial one becomes modest. It works for any Intelligence goal, persuasion or planning alike: the letter is the groundwork already laid.

**Watch.** The cost is social and lasting: every use is done in someone else's name, and the GM can call in the debt. Survival is excluded because its cost is locked when it triggers.

## Madstone — Weird trinket

> *A smooth stone from a white deer's belly; pressed to a wound, it clings until the harm is drawn out.*
> Wake: Boil it in fresh milk until the milk runs clear, and carry it against your skin for a day.
> When: Your approach is Weird.
> Effect: Pressed to a bite, sting, or poisoned wound, it clings until the venom is out. At cleanup, choose 1 consequence status you would gain and gain none of it.
> Cost: The stone will not cling again until you boil it in fresh milk; the milk turns green with what it drew.
> *You carry the faint sour smell of boiled milk.*

**In the fiction.** Real Appalachian folk medicine. Draw out a copperhead's venom before the leg swells, save a child bitten by a rabid dog, pull the poison from a well-meant cup. Families lent their madstone to neighbours from miles away, so owning one is a standing in the hollow as much as a cure.

**At the table.** The only item that touches failure consequences: after a failed check, one status never arrives. It cannot prevent death, because survival failure is not a status.

**Watch.** No status Cost, because paying a status to cancel a status is a wash; the limit is the boiling and the fresh milk each time. Grown from the Witch Bottle, whose effect it keeps with new fiction.

## Hag Stone — Weird trinket

> *A stone with a hole worn clean through it; looked through, it shows the true shape of things.*
> Wake: Find it yourself in running water, and look through it at the sun until your eye waters.
> When: Your approach is Weird.
> Effect: Through it, a disguise, a glamour, or a borrowed face shows what wears it. Before a lead, look through it at another player's hand, and tell no one what you saw. Outside a check, read a relic's price before it is invoked.
> Cost: Gain 1 Stress. Whatever you looked at can see you too; the GM decides who noticed.
> *One of your eyes never quite focuses on the person in front of you.*

**In the fiction.** A town that knows the Hollow-Skinned exist lives on suspicion. The stone answers the question everyone is afraid to ask: is that really your neighbour? It also spots fae work and glamour, and reads what a relic will cost before anyone pays it.

**At the table.** The only way to know an ally's cards: one-way and silent, so it sharpens independent play without breaking the no-signals rule.

**Watch.** Reading a relic's price in advance takes the edge off relics' hidden cost; it costs a Stress and a watcher each time.

## Bindle — Dexterity trinket

> *A traveller's bindle that always holds the one thing its owner turns out to need, if they could have packed it.*
> Wake: Pack everything you own into it, and walk away from the last place you slept.
> Effect: Between checks, untie one ordinary thing you could have packed. At cleanup, tie up to 2 cards from your hand into it; after any later deal, untie them into hand.
> Cost: Gain 1 Stumble.
> *You never quite unpack, wherever you stay.*

**In the fiction.** On the ridge road, reach in and find the rope, the lamp, the dry socks, the letter of introduction you are sure you packed. Drifters, peddlers, and anyone one step ahead of a debt carry one. It improvises; it does not lighten a load.

**At the table.** Every other hand effect ends with its check; the bindle holds up to two cards for as long as you like and hands them back after any later deal. Tied cards sit outside discard, so they miss reshuffles and survival scans. Extra cards add no tricks.

**Watch.** No approach gate, because it bridges checks. The Wake means leaving somewhere for good, so the GM can refuse it. It puts Dexterity at five trinkets.

Second pass: the first version also made everything you own weigh no more than a loaf. That swallowed the Porter's Rope, which makes one carryable burden weightless, so the bindle now improvises and the rope keeps weight.

## Bone Whistle — Strength trinket

> *A herder's bone whistle whose call brings strays home to the flock, or cuts one loose from it.*
> Wake: Call a lost animal home across a ridge with it, and walk it back yourself.
> When: Your approach is Strength.
> Effect: Its call carries across a hollow: bring a stray, beast or person, home to you, or part one from a crowd without a fuss. Before drawing, deal the world one card more or one fewer.
> Cost: Gain 1 Stumble.
> *Dogs and stock watch you as if waiting for a signal.*

**In the fiction.** The Tarran herders' tool, and anyone's who works stock on the ridges. Whistle a strayed cow off a cliff edge, call a lost child home through the fog, or cut one troublemaker out of a crowd at the courthouse steps and send them walking. Both directions are useful: sometimes you want the flock gathered, sometimes one of it gone.

**At the table.** One card fewer and the world runs dry a trick early, sitting out the last one. One card more and the world is likelier to hold the led suit, so it follows more and trumps in less, though its spare card might be a high one. Choosing reads the check: fewer when the world looks strong, more when it looks short of suits.

**Watch.** Chosen before drawing, before anyone has seen the world's row, so it is a read of the situation rather than a certain gain. The Stumble is the chase after the flock. Strength fits herding by command; Weird, for beasts answering the call, is the alternative, though that leaves Strength a trinket short.

## Hand of Glory — Dexterity relic

> *A candle dipped around a hanged man's hand; while it burns, no sleeper wakes unless its light falls on them.*
> Wake: Light it from the hearth of a house where everyone sleeps.
> When: Your approach is Dexterity.
> Effect: While it burns, no sleeper can be woken, but anyone its light touches wakes at once, so you work in the dark. Before a lead, shield its light from the opposition: the world sits out that trick and the next, and you lead any trick it would.
> *Sleepers turn their faces away from you.*
> Reveal its price.

> Price: At cleanup, gain 1 Injury. The flame burns down to your own fingers.
> Price: Each use, the GM names one sleeper who woke in its light and saw your face.
> *You cannot warm your hands at any fire.*

**In the fiction.** Walk a condemned friend out of the county jail past a sleeping sheriff, as long as you never hold the light up to his face. Take back what a creditor seized, feeling your way through a house full of sleepers. The candle helps only those willing to move blind: the moment you need to see, you risk waking someone.

**At the table.** For two tricks the opposition is simply absent: players contest only each other, and the world scores nothing.

**Watch.** Still the strongest of the six. The Injury makes each use a survival risk, and every use leaves a witness the GM chooses.

Second pass: the lighter no longer has to be someone falling asleep, which was a big ask. The limit is now the light itself: it wakes whoever it touches, which keeps the fiction narrow and makes the witness price follow naturally.

## Backward Watch — Intelligence relic

> *A pocket watch that, wound backward, takes back the last few moments for everyone but its holder.*
> Wake: Stop it at the moment something goes wrong, and carry it stopped until that hour comes round again.
> When: Your approach is Intelligence.
> Effect: Wind it back to unsay a word or catch a fall; only you remember how it went. After a trick, before its status effects and consolations, wind it back: every card played returns to its owner's hand, and the trick is played again from the same lead.
> *You check the hour more often than anyone needs to.*
> Reveal its price.

> Price: At cleanup, gain 1 Stress.
> Price: You keep the time you took back. Each use ages you a season; the GM says where it shows.
> *Clocks near you run a little slow.*

**In the fiction.** Take back the insult that started the feud, catch the lamp before the barn catches, hear the answer to your proposal and try again with better words. Only the holder remembers the first way it went.

**At the table.** The one exception to a card never coming back. Everyone replays the trick knowing what they know now; the world, playing its leftmost legal card, usually plays the same card again, so the rewind mostly favours the players.

**Watch.** The Wake needs a real moment gone wrong and a whole day carrying the stopped watch. Ageing is a price the table can see accumulate.

## Handfasting Cord — Weird relic

> *A plaited cord that, tied round two joined hands, binds the pair so each feels what the other feels.*
> Wake: Tie it round your hand and another's before a witness, and both speak a vow.
> When: Your approach is Weird.
> Effect: Each of you always knows where the other is and whether they are hurt. This check, your wins also score for your bound partner's goal, and theirs for yours.
> *The two of you reach for the same cup.*
> Reveal its price.

> Price: While the cord binds you, every status either of you gains, the other gains too.
> Price: The bond ends only when one of you dies, or both agree to cut it; cutting it costs each of you 1 Injury.
> *When your partner is hurt, you flinch first.*

**In the fiction.** Handfasting is the hollow's oldest marriage, and the cord is the kind that takes. Find your partner anywhere in the hills, feel the moment they are hurt, and never be truly alone in a fight. Siblings, sworn friends, and lovers all use it.

**At the table.** The one exception to wins never being pooled. For a check, two players score together: two goals that each looked out of reach can both land.

**Watch.** The shared-status price is lasting and symmetrical: every Injury, Curse, and Stress one partner gains, the other gains too, until death or an Injury-costly parting.

## Set-aside drafts

Earlier rounds, one line each, in case one is worth reviving:

- **Surveyor's Chain** (Intelligence trinket): raise or lower the check's N by 1. Set aside as too specific to boundaries; its dispute idea became the Tally Stick.
- **Holdout Sleeve** (Dexterity trinket): keep one card between checks. Reworked into the Bindle.
- **Commonplace Book** (Intelligence relic): one card climbs a value at each rest.
- **Saint's Knuckle** (Weird relic): the next card you play counts as Blessed.
- **Fair-Copy Pen** (Intelligence trinket): move one discard card to the bottom between checks, so an Injury leaves the scan window.
- **Witch Bottle** (Weird trinket): cancel one consequence status at cleanup; the harm rebounds on its sender. Its effect lives on in the Madstone, with new fiction.
- **Slip Knot** (Dexterity trinket): play last in a trick and ignore follow-suit.
- **Weathering Almanac** (Intelligence relic): arrange the world's row after the deal.
- **Mourning Veil** (Weird relic): switch your personal trump to the led suit mid-check.
- **Cutpurse Glove** (Dexterity relic): swap a card from your hand with an unplayed world card.
